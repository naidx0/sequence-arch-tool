Sheet 07 · The board

Modules, nesting and containment

A repository is not flat and Sequence's schema has never pretended it was. ArchNode.parentId and SeqDiagramGroup.memberIds both ship today, and today the board draws neither — presentKindFor() maps module to service, and the children of a node are flattened into a list of names printed on its card face. A list of names is not a picture of containment. It is a picture of a card that knows something and will not show you.

This sheet makes module a first-class kind, gives containment a real drawn form, and states the ceiling on how deep it goes. It closes with the law the rest of it stands on: containment is read from the graph, never inferred from a name, a path or a layout position — and topology is never fabricated to make a container look busier than it is.

07.1

Module is a kind, not a spelling of Service

six kinds · six icons · six silhouettes · no hue

The board has six kinds and Module is one of them. Its silhouette is a folder: an --r-14 body with a tab (--arch-tab-w by --arch-tab-h) drawn above its top-left corner in the card's own border style, weight and ink, and carrying no bottom edge. The tab is drawn rather than declared because a corner radius could not have carried it: CSS scales every corner by f = min(side length / the radii summed on that side), so below a certain size --r-14 and --r-18 paint identically and the difference the token asserted is discarded. A drawn tab is not scaled away. The tone is recessive (--surface-1) because a container that sits in front of its own contents is inside out, and the border is dashed at --w-struct: dashed means a boundary drawn around things, which is the whole of what a module is.

Kinds Service Module Datastore Topic Agent Position Entry

Six rows in two groups — five buildable kinds, then the entry position, which sheet 03 §03.7 says may not stand among them. The legend the board ships today has five rows, one flat list, and no Module at all, which is why a reader can look at a board of a monorepo and never learn that it has modules in it.

Every architecture node kind must remain distinguishable with all colour removed, at every size the silhouette is drawn, from the radii that actually paint. The last clause is the load-bearing one: CSS scales every corner by f = min(side length / the radii summed on that side), so a declared radius is not a rendered radius. Module differs from Service in icon, in silhouette (a drawn folder tab against a plain rounded rectangle — both bodies are --r-14, and the tab is the part that cannot be scaled away), in border style (dashed against solid), in weight and in tone — five channels, none of them colour. A file drawn as a card would differ from Module in none of the five, which is the whole reason a file is not a kind on this board.
07.2

The container is a wall, and the members are inside it

--nest-1 · --r-18 · --sp-4 inset · 24px header

A module with drawn members is not a card. It is a .nest — a wall with a header and its members laid out inside it. The wall keeps the module's icon and the module's dashed --w-struct border, so a reader who saw the collapsed card recognises the open one as the same object rather than a second one.

Two decisions in the wall's geometry are arithmetic rather than taste. The wall is --r-18 and its inset is --sp-4, so a member lands on --r-14 — which is exactly the service card's own silhouette. Radii nest correctly by subtraction, never by eye. And the header is 24px because that is the height Graphite already gives .chip, .btn.sm and .railhead. Containment does not get a new rung on the height ladder.

07.3

Collapsed and expanded are one object in two states

--seq-dur-panel · the count never disappears

A module is expanded when its members are drawn and collapsed when they are not. Which state a module is in is a fact about the picture, never a fact about the graph — collapsing hides drawing, it does not hide truth. The member count therefore appears in both states, in the wall header when open and in the card footer when shut, so a collapsed module can never be mistaken for an empty one.

The disclosure control is the header itself, not a button parked on it: the whole 24px strip is the hit target and the chevron is a 12px glyph riding at its right end beside the count. That keeps the header at 24px rather than the 26px an .iconbtn would force, and it means the target grows with the wall instead of staying a 26px square in the corner of a 340px object.

The leading icon slot belongs to the kind in every state, and the chevron may never take it. Under Decision 2 the icon is the primary carrier of kind; a module whose glyph has been replaced by a chevron has traded the channel that identifies it for the channel that says whether it is open, and open is the less important of the two facts.

07.4

What happens to an edge that crosses a wall

four cases · a wall never owns an edge

There are exactly four cases, and the rule behind all four is one sentence: a wall never owns an edge. An edge drawn touching a wall is always standing in for an edge belonging to something inside it. There is no such thing as the module calls the database unless the analyzer traced a call from a member, and if it did, the edge is the member's.

07.5

Depth stops at three, and the ceiling is stated

--nest-0 · --nest-1 · --nest-2 · --nest-3

Containment depth is carried by tone, one step per level, and the ramp has four rungs. The board spends three of them — ground, wall, member — and the fourth is the ceiling. There is no --nest-4, so a fourth wall would have to reuse a tone that is already spoken for, and the picture would stop saying anything about depth at exactly the point a reader most needs it to.

The ramp

Four steps, zero chroma. Each box is the surface a thing at that depth sits on.

--nest-0 · the ground
--nest-1 · a wall
--nest-2 · a member
--nest-3 · the ceiling

Radii shrink with the ramp — 14, 18 minus its inset, 14, 10 — because a nested box with the same corner as its parent reads as a box that has escaped.

A module inside a module

The inner module does not get a wall. It gets a card and a count, and opening it drills in — the outer wall becomes the ground and the ramp resets to zero.

Why a ceiling is better than a fallback. An undeclared limit is discovered by whoever opens the deepest repository, and it is discovered as a bug. Writing the ceiling down turns the fourth level from a rendering failure into a navigation affordance — the thing the reader gets instead is a drill-in, which is better than a fourth wall would have been anyway.
07.6

L1, L2, L3 — and a level only exists when it differs

a filter that hides real nodes must say what it hid

The levels are a standing filter over one graph, not three graphs. L1 shows the things a reader would name if asked what the system is: entry points, services and agents. L2 adds every scanned participant — datastores, topics and modules. L3 shows the same nodes as L2 and opens them: route labels appear on edges, and a module's members are drawn as real nodes inside its wall rather than flattened into a list of names printed on its card face. That last clause is the whole reason L3 was worth unpinning.

Opening a module does not promote its files and its functions to cards. They are interior detail, and they are reported in the parts strip sheet 02 specifies — #ic-fn and a count in .nd-ft — then enumerated in the index rail. Drawing one as a card would mean drawing it with Module's icon, Module's dashed border, Module's tone and Module's silhouette, and a seventh kind that renders byte-identically to the sixth is not a kind. It is the greyscale invariant failing inside the book that states it.

Every level below L2 hides nodes that exist. A filter that hides real nodes has to say so on the board, in the same breath as the counts it is showing — otherwise L1 reads as a claim that the system has four parts.

Levels are a filter, and a filter keeps its coordinates. Changing level changes which nodes are drawn, which changes the layout, which moves the camera. 07.7

Containment is read. Topology is never fabricated.

parentId · memberIds · and nothing else

A wall is drawn around a set of nodes for exactly two reasons: they share an ArchNode.parentId, or they appear in one SeqDiagramGroup.memberIds. Not because their labels share a prefix. Not because their paths share a folder. Not because the layout engine happened to put them near each other. A drawn boundary is a claim that the graph asserts the boundary, and if the graph does not, the boundary is a guess wearing the costume of a fact.

The second half of the law is stronger and it is where the board is currently wrong. Members are a set, not a sequence. Opening a node's parts today synthesises a chain — part-0 to part-1 to part-2 — and hands the reader a drawn arrow that no analyzer ever observed. Every edge in this product carries evidence; the schema says so in its own header. An edge with no evidence record is not a stylistic liberty, it is a schema violation. 

for (let i = 0; i < nodes.length - 1; i += 1) {
  edges.push({ from, to, family: 'control' })
}
// three parts in, two arrows out, zero evidence
nodes = members.map(m => ({ ...m, parentId: node.id }))
edges = graph.edges.filter(hasEvidence)
// three parts in, three members out, no arrows

Handback — one measured finding. .nest separates from the board ground by tone alone at 1.09:1, and its only other carrier is a dashed --edge border, which resolves to 1.21:1 against that ground and is then broken into dashes. That is quieter than the node-card defect this migration exists to fix, which was measured at 1.11:1 — and the wall is the most important structural object on the board. The fix is one token, not a new class: --edge-strong on .nest takes the boundary to 1.51:1 while staying entirely neutral, which is the same move the substrate already makes on .empty for the same reason — a dashed border needs more ink than a solid one to read as the same line. Raised here rather than patched locally.
Handback — two implementation notes. First, .nest-bd is flex-wrap:row and a column of members needs flex-direction:column set at the call site; if column members turn out to be the common case on a real board, that belongs in the substrate as a modifier rather than in twenty inline styles. Second, module and package share one silhouette and one icon by decision and are separated only by the mono .nd-k tag — which means they are told apart by reading and not at zoom. That is the correct trade for two container kinds, and it is the only place on this sheet where the greyscale test passes on a tag rather than on a shape. file and function are outside that trade entirely: they are interior detail rather than board kinds, so they never take a card, never enter the legend and never enter the pair matrix. A tag is a thin enough channel for two; it is not one at all for a seventh kind that would otherwise be pixel-for-pixel a Module.
