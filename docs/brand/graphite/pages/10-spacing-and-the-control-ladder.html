Sheet 10 · Foundations

The space scale and the control ladder

Two closed sets. Twelve steps of space, ten rungs of height, and no thirteenth step and no eleventh rung. Every gap, every padding, every control, every row and every icon in Sequence lands on a value declared here — and a value that is not on the ladder is a defect, not a preference. There is no argument from “it looked cramped”, because the ladder is the argument.

Compact is the only density and it is drawn at compact, never scaled down from a comfortable one. The working type sizes are 10 / 11 / 12px and 10px is the floor — 10.8 states what that forbids.

10.1

The space scale — twelve steps, closed

4px base · named for pixels · inherited from Graphite unchanged

Each step is named for the number of pixels it is, so there is no arithmetic between reading --sp-10 and knowing it is ten. Eight of the twelve steps are 12px or under: that distribution is compact. The scale spends its resolution where components are built and only gets coarse once it is separating whole sections of a page.

The scale, at size

The bars are the length they name and carry no radius — a ruler with rounded ends lies about where it ends.

Re-checked against the substrate as it now stands. Twelve steps, unchanged — the repair that rebuilt the six silhouettes added geometry rather than spacing, and every dimension it added landed on this scale without a new step being cut: --arch-tab-w is --sp-32, --arch-tab-h is --sp-12, and the agent chamfer takes its bite at --sp-16. .zoomcluster, which used to write a raw 2px twice, now writes --sp-2. The claim holds for every gap and every padding between things. It does not hold below --sp-2, where three shipped surfaces need an inset the scale cannot name — that is the second handback, booked in 10.10.

10.2

The gap ladder

five values inside components · the rest is page furniture

A gap is chosen by what it separates, not by how the result feels. Inside a component the gap is one of five values and never more than --sp-12. --sp-16 separates whole blocks — the turns in a transcript, the legend row of a chart frame. Anything above --sp-16 is page furniture and may not appear inside a component at all.

10.4

The row ladder

44 · 38 · 30 · 28 · 26

Row height is semantic, not decorative. A tool call is 26px because it is a footnote; a run is 38px because it carries two lines and a delta; a bar is 44px because it is chrome and chrome is the only thing on screen allowed to be that tall. Three of the five rungs are shared with the control ladder — 28, 26 and 30 — which is what makes a button sit flush in a rail beside a thread row without a single one-off adjustment.

10.5

Icon buttons and glyph sizes

three button sizes · five glyph sizes · one stroke weight

An icon button is a square on the control ladder, and there are exactly three of them: 26px is the default, 24px is the composer’s, 20px is the one that lives inside a header strip where a 26px square would be taller than the strip. The glyph inside is picked by the square, not by taste. The stroke is 1.5 at every size and is never scaled — that is what makes a 12px icon look like the same family as a 20px one.

10.6

The inset ladder — horizontal padding

three values and two named exceptions

Horizontal padding takes exactly three values. Nothing in Sequence is inset --sp-4, nothing is inset 5px, and nothing is inset --sp-16. Vertical padding on a control does not exist at all: a control has a height, and its content is centred in it.

.toolrow is a 26px row inset --sp-10, not --sp-8, because .toolstack pulls it left by exactly --sp-10 so the tool name lands on the transcript measure. The inset is load-bearing; changing it breaks the alignment it exists for.

Exception 2 — named

Unknown Both are 22px

.verdict is a 22px pill inset --sp-10 where .tooltip at the same height takes --sp-8. A verdict is uppercase mono tracked at .08em, and tracked mono needs one step more or the last letter’s sidebearing collapses into the --r-full curve.

Two exceptions, both written down, both with a reason a reader can check on the screen. A third exception without a reason is a defect — the point of naming these two is that the list is now closed.

10.7

Compact is the only density

airy is a defect, and this is what it costs

Sequence is a tool for reading a repository. The rail holds folders and threads, the pane holds a file and its facts, and the board holds a graph. Every pixel of row height is a row the reader cannot see. There is no comfortable mode and no density toggle: the design system that shipped two row-size modes and defaulted to the looser one is exactly what Graphite deleted.

10.8

The type floor

--t-10 · 10px · and nothing below it, ever

10px is the floor. No label, no legend, no edge plaque, no badge, no axis tick and no caption renders below --t-10. This is not a guideline: a board can be zoomed out, and the answer to a label that will not fit at 10px is to drop the label, not to shrink it. The edge plaque in this book was 9px in the running app and was raised to --t-10 for exactly that reason.

The working band

Three sizes carry almost the whole interface. Each pairs with a line-height token — a literal line-height is the same defect as a literal size.

The line ramp is as closed as the size ramp — and it absorbs all three

The ramp holds exactly nine distinct line values — 14, 15, 16, 18, 21, 22, 23, 26 and 30, one per size, with 22 sitting below 23 because --t-17 is a heading and a heading sets tighter than the prose under it. The one real exception, and it is a pairing, not a number

.codeblock and .diffwrap set --t-11 on an 18px line, where the table above pairs 11px with 15px.

four lines of mono, each set at --t-11
the line under this one sits 18px away
at --lh-11 it would sit 15px away, and
the eye loses the next line's left edge

18px is --lh-13 — on the ramp, two steps up from the size’s own partner. It is a deliberate pairing and the rule behind it is narrow enough to close: a mono block read line by line takes the line of the prose it sits inside, not the line of its own size. A code block and a diff are read one line at a time and the eye has to find the next line’s left edge; 15px on 11px mono, which is wider per character than the sans, closes that gap to almost nothing. This applies to .codeblock and .diffwrap and to nothing else — an inline .mono span inside a row takes the row’s line, and a mono label takes its own. Two blocks, one rule, and the list is closed the same way 10.6’s two inset exceptions are.

The floor is a zoom rule too. The board scales with the camera, so a node title at --t-12 is under 10px the moment the camera drops below 84%. Below that threshold a node sheds its subtitle, then its kind label, then its title — and keeps its icon, its silhouette, its border treatment and its tone, which is precisely why Decision 2 puts kind on four non-chromatic channels rather than on a label. Nothing is ever rendered small. It is dropped.

10.10

A value not on the ladder is a defect

the test, and the two handbacks this sheet raises
Do

Three controls, one rung, one inset. They were not tuned against each other — they were each set to --control-h and --sp-10 and then they matched.

Don’t

32, 30 and 26 — three heights that each felt right in isolation and none of which is on the ladder for a control. There is no rule anyone can apply next time, and the next screen will invent three more.

The test. For any new surface: read the height off the ladder, read the inset off 10.6, read the gap off 10.2, read the glyph size off 10.5. If none of them fits, the shape is wrong — not the ladder. Raise it as a handback and add a rung deliberately, once, with a reason. Six local inventions are six contradictions.

Handback 1 to the substrate — six unnamed rungs. Six of the ten rungs on the height ladder — 38, 24, 22, 20, 16 and 14 — are literals in _core.html with no token name, while 44, 30, 28 and 26 have one. A ladder whose rungs are half-named is a ladder that will grow an eleventh rung by accident, because there is nothing to write instead. This sheet specifies the closed set and asks the substrate to name the remaining six. Until it does, the rungs are documented here and nowhere else, which is the weakest link in this sheet.
Handback 2 to the substrate — the scale has no rung below --sp-2, and three surfaces need one. 10.1 says twelve steps, closed, and after the substrate repair that is still true of every gap and every padding between two things. It is not true of the inset inside a small control, where 1px survives and cannot be collapsed upward. The switch is the case that proves it: the track is 16px tall with a --w-hair border on each edge, so its padding box is 14px, and the knob is 12px positioned 1px in. Move that inset to --sp-2 and the knob is either 10px in a 14px box — a visibly different control — or 12px overflowing its own track. The same 1px is load-bearing on .ph, which holds its dotted underline 1px clear of its text, and on .thread .dot. And 10.9’s own replacement for .inline-code is written 1px var(--sp-4) — a row in the retirement table that still carries a raw literal, because there is nothing to write instead. This is not the same defect as a 1px gap. The three 1px gaps in 10.9 are real density defects and they collapse to --sp-2 correctly: two stacked lines are a cluster and a cluster is gapped --sp-2. A 1px inset is arithmetic, not taste. The radius ramp already met this exact problem and answered it in the right place — --r-bar is a named, justified, single-purpose sub-4px step, declared with its reason beside it. The space scale needs the same: either one named sub-2px step with the same discipline, or a written ruling that a 1px inset is a hairline of ground and belongs to the stroke-width set rather than the space set. Until one of those is decided, this sheet cannot honestly claim the scale is closed for insets, and it does not.
