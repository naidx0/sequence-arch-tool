Sheet 05 · The board

The canvas: field, camera and gesture

Sequence is one live canvas. Everything else — the chat on the left, the file and functions rail on the right — is chrome around it. So the canvas has to behave like a canvas, and the test for that is not a feature list. It is whether the ground moves with the content. A field that stays nailed down while the content slides across it is the single biggest tell that a board is not a real board, and it is what the shipped board does today: the grid layer is a sibling of the transformed layer and nothing ever writes its position or its size.

This sheet decides four things and nothing else: what the field is, what the camera does when you scroll, what the one control cluster looks like and where it lives, and what the pointer is allowed to say. Node kinds, edges and containment are Decision 2's territory and are specified on their own sheets — but the canvas leans on Decision 2 harder than anything else in the product, because below a certain zoom the labels are gone and the silhouette is the only thing left.

05.1

The field lives inside the transform

--board-field-pitch · --board-field-dot · --board-field-ink

The field is a dot grid at --board-field-pitch, one --board-field-dot wide, inked at --board-field-ink. It is not a backdrop. It is drawn in the same coordinate space as the nodes, which means the camera moves it: pan writes background-position, zoom writes background-size, and both stay locked to the content layer's own translate and scale. The dots are how a reader senses motion and distance; if they do not move, the board feels like a picture being dragged behind a window.

The ink is --edge, not --viz-grid: a one-pixel dot at a 24px pitch carries far less weight than a continuous hairline, and --edge is where the two match perceptually.

05.2

Exactly one field, and never two

one background-image · one pitch

The shipped board draws two: a 24px dot grid and a 26px line grid, on separate layers, one static and one tracking. Two pitches two pixels apart do not add detail — they produce a moiré that drifts as the camera moves, and the eye spends its attention resolving a pattern that means nothing. There is one field. If a second grid is ever wanted, it replaces the first; it does not join it.

Do

One dot field at one pitch. Flat, quiet, and it disappears the moment there is a card on top of it.

Don't

A dot field at one pitch under a line grid two pixels off it. The beat is visible standing still and crawls when the camera moves.

05.3

Scroll pans. Ctrl or Cmd plus scroll zooms.

one camera · both surfaces · no per-surface variants

This is the reversal of what the board ships. Today a plain wheel maps straight into zoom with no modifier branch, and a two-finger scroll never pans at all — which means the most common gesture on a trackpad is bound to the most destructive camera operation. Miro and Figma both do the opposite, and so does every board a Sequence user has used before this one. Plain scroll pans in both axes; the modifier is what buys you zoom.

Zoom about the pointer, not about the viewport centre, for every continuous zoom. The stepped zooms in 05.6 use the viewport centre, because a button press has no pointer position on the board.

This departs from the library default. React Flow ships zoomOnScroll: true and panOnScroll: false, and the Architecture surface takes both unchanged. Both are overridden here. A library default is not a design decision, and the two shipped surfaces currently disagree with each other on this exact pair — which is the real cost: the same gesture means two things depending on which half of the app you are in.
05.4

The range: a quarter to four, and where both ends come from

reciprocal about 1:1

A zoom range is a design constant, so it has to be derived rather than picked. Both ends fall out of token arithmetic, and they are reciprocals, which means the readout is symmetric about 1:1 — two doublings out, two doublings in.

The shipped board sets minZoom to 0.1, on both surfaces — NativeBoard.tsx:430 and Canvas.tsx:275. At that floor an architecture card's title, 13.5px from --card-title-size, paints at 1.35 device pixels, and the 11px sub-row paints at 1.1. Neither is a small label; both are a smear. The floor above exists so that the level-of-detail ladder in 05.8, not the reader's eyesight, is what decides when a label goes. And the ceiling has to move the other way to adopt this range. Both surfaces cap maxZoom at 2.5 (NativeBoard.tsx:431, Canvas.tsx:276), which is below the 4.00 derived above, so this sheet does not describe the shipped camera: taking it means raising both caps to 4. That is a code change, said plainly here rather than left for someone to find when the zoom-in button stops responding two stops early.

05.5

One fit policy, shared by every surface

inset --sp-24 · ceiling 1.00 · --seq-dur-cam

Fit and zoom-to-selection are the same operation with different bounds, so they are one function with one policy. Half of that is already shipped and this sheet ratifies it rather than proposing it: packages/web/src/canvas/fit.ts declares FIT_PADDING = 0.2 and FIT_MAX_ZOOM = 1, bundles them as FIT_OPTS, and both surfaces spread that one bundle (NativeBoard.tsx:440, Canvas.tsx:285). One ceiling, one padding, two canvases.

What has not converged is the other end, and it is a real divergence rather than an argument for one. The same file declares two auto-fit floors in its own two option bundles: DESIGN_FIT_MIN_ZOOM = 0.45 (fit.ts:44) for the design canvas and ARCH_FIT_MIN_ZOOM = 0.6 (fit.ts:71) for the Architecture board. Each is justified in place by the smallest type on its own surface — which is exactly why they should not differ: under 05.8 the type floor is one number for the whole product, so the fit floor is one number derived from it, not two derived from two card designs. Fit clamps to the range's own floor and pans the remainder. The second divergence is the inset itself: the shipped padding is a fraction of the viewport, so the gutter grows with the window, and this sheet specifies device pixels instead, for the reason under the specimens below.

05.6

One control cluster, in one corner, in one language

.zoomcluster · bottom right · --sp-12 from both edges

The board ships two clusters in two corners in two languages: React Flow's own untokened controls bottom-left on Architecture, overlapping the kind legend, and a hand-built bottom-right cluster on Canvas. Neither shows a percentage, so the reader can pan and zoom for a minute and have no idea where the camera is. There is one cluster. It is .zoomcluster, it sits bottom-right at --sp-12 from both edges, and it always carries the readout.

Fit ships as a word, never as ic-frame — Sheet 09, ruling 3: ic-frame means the boundary drawn around a selection, and fitting the board to the viewport draws no boundary. The icon vocabulary has no glyph for a camera move and does not borrow one. Zoom to selection is not a fifth control either. It is a keystroke and a menu item — Frame selection — because it is meaningless without a selection, and a permanently half-dead control in a four-control cluster is worse than no control.

05.7

The pointer says what the next click will do

grab when selecting · crosshair only when drawing

A cursor is a promise. The shipped board applies its draw class unconditionally and it wins on source order over cursor: grab, so the pointer says “you are about to draw” while the only gesture available is a pan. There are four cursors on the canvas and each one is bound to a state the reader can verify by moving the mouse two pixels.

05.8

Labels are hidden at low zoom. They are never shrunk.

the type floor is absolute, in device pixels

Working type is 10 / 11 / 12px and 10px is the floor. On a canvas the camera multiplies that, so a rung authored at --t-N renders at N × zoom device pixels — and the moment that product drops below the floor, the rung is hidden. It is not scaled, not substituted for a smaller size, and not replaced by an ellipsis. Every threshold below is --t-10 divided by the rung's own authored size; there is no new number in the ladder at all.

The last card carries no text, no icon and no hue, and it is still a service and not a datastore. That is Decision 2 paying for itself. A design that spent kind on colour would have nothing left at this rung — and a design that spent it on hue matching the status vocabulary, as the shipped board does, would be showing the reader a wall of “passed” and “warning” at the exact zoom where they can no longer read the labels that would have corrected them.

This is stricter than Miro and Figma, deliberately. Those boards scale their type down well past 10px before hiding it, because their smallest canvas type starts far above the floor and has room to fall. Graphite's working type is already at its floor at 1:1, so it has none. The consequence is real and worth stating rather than discovering: edge tags and provenance are 1:1 detail, and a reader who wants to read the labels on a large graph pans at 1:1 rather than zooming out to see everything at once. The alternative — the 1.35px title the shipped floor of 0.1 produces, from 05.4 — is not a smaller label, and reading it is not a skill.
05.9

What a board must do to be a board

every line here is testable

Nine assertions. Each is checkable by driving the camera and reading a computed style, which is the point: a board that is not a board fails these silently and looks fine in a screenshot.

#Assertion
1The field's background-position changes when the camera pans, and its background-size scales with zoom.
2There is exactly one background-image field layer on the board, at exactly one pitch.
3A wheel event with no modifier changes the pan offset and does not change zoom.
4A wheel event with ctrlKey or metaKey changes zoom and holds the point under the pointer fixed.
5Zoom is clamped to 0.25 … 4.00 on every path — gesture, button, key and fit alike.
6Fit and zoom-to-selection call one function with one inset and one ceiling, and fit never returns a zoom above 1.00.
7There is one .zoomcluster in the document, it is in the bottom-right corner, and it renders a percentage.
8The computed cursor is grab when the tool is select and the pointer is over empty ground, and crosshair only when the draw tool is armed.
9No text node on the board renders below --t-10 in device pixels at any zoom in the range.

Assertion 9 is the one that will be argued with. Keep it. Every other rule on this sheet is a preference that a reasonable person could hold differently; that one is the difference between a label and a smudge, and the ladder in 05.8 is how it is met without ever hiding something the reader still needs.
