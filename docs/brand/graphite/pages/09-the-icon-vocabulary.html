Sheet 09 · Primitives

One glyph, one meaning

Seventy-six symbols, one stroke weight, one grid — and, from this sheet forward, one written meaning each. Graphite gave Sequence the drawings and the discipline that makes them a set. It did not give the product a table saying what each one claims, and that omission is where the reuse came from: a glyph with no written meaning acquires one every time somebody reaches for it.

The law is short. One glyph, one written meaning; an icon that means two things means neither. Its corollary is the half people forget: two glyphs may never mean the same thing. Both failures are live in the product today — one drawing carrying eleven meanings, and one meaning carrying three drawings — and both are fixed by the same table, not by drawing more.

09.1

The law, and the two ways it breaks

both specimens are reported defects

The test is not whether a glyph appears twice — ic-check legitimately means passed, accepted and done, because those are one claim wearing three words. The test is whether one drawing stands in for two things a reader must tell apart. When it does, the reader has to already know the answer to read the screen, which is the definition of an interface that does not work.

The second failure is the mirror of the first and is harder to see, because every individual screen looks fine. One meaning wearing several drawings costs the reader the ability to recognise the same act twice — and it is worst exactly where recognition matters most, on the control that writes to disk.

09.2

How one is drawn

24px grid · 1.5px · round caps and joins

Inherited from Graphite unchanged, and restated here because it is half of what makes seventy-six drawings one vocabulary. Every symbol is authored on a 24×24 grid and rendered through .i, which sets the stroke, the caps, the joins and the size in one place. The glyph carries no stroke width and no colour of its own, so a symbol cannot drift from the set by being used somewhere new.

The one rule that makes them a set

Declared once, on the class, inherited by all seventy-six.

.i{
  width:16px; height:16px; flex:0 0 auto;
  stroke:currentColor; fill:none;
  stroke-width:1.5;
  stroke-linecap:round; stroke-linejoin:round;
}

A glyph that sets its own stroke-width or its own fill is a defect, not a variant. There are exactly two sanctioned exceptions and they are named and drawn in §09.6. No emoji, at any size, on any surface, ever.

09.4

The complete vocabulary

glyph · the one meaning · where it may appear

Every remaining symbol, drawn at .i, with the single meaning it carries and the surfaces it is allowed on. The third line is binding. A glyph outside its listed surfaces is a new meaning being minted quietly, which is precisely how one drawing came to carry eleven of them. Adding a surface to a row is a change to this sheet, made deliberately, and never a decision taken at a call site.

09.5

Held — drawn, and allowed nowhere

inherited from a product Sequence is not

Ten of the inherited drawings name things Sequence does not have: hardware feasibility, datasets, train/eval splits, hyperparameters. They stay in the sprite because deleting them loses the drawing and the discipline that made it, but they are allowed on no surface at all — and a held glyph is the most tempting thing in the vocabulary, because it looks free. It is not. Borrowing one is exactly how a drawing acquires a second meaning.

ic-gauge Diagnose, feasibility. held — no surface
ic-dataset A dataset, a table. held — no surface

A held glyph is a drawing with no surface. This is the opposite: a surface with no drawing. The answer is the one §09.1 gave Save.

The five tools boardTools.ts exports, in its order, with its shortcuts. Decision 8 closed Erase; Decision 9 closed Pan (and the whiteboard's Box tool as ic-rect) through §09.8. Arrow still ships as a word — the nearest glyph is ic-arrowright, which already means next in the book shell, and borrowing it would mint a second meaning on a drawing that has one. Both are already spoken for. Settings is ic-gear; diagnosis, if it ever gets a surface, gets a justification and a row before it gets a drawing.
09.6

One stroke weight, and its two sanctioned exceptions

76 symbols · 1 filled · 3 cap-dots

One weight for the whole set, declared on .i and never overridden. Two constructions in the sprite do not fit that sentence literally, and both are drawn here rather than hidden, because an undocumented exception is how a set stops being a set. There is no third.

Exception 1 — ic-more is filled

The only symbol carrying fill="currentColor" and stroke="none".

ic-more · filled
ic-commit · stroked

Its three dots are radius 0.9 on the 24-unit grid. A 1.5-wide ring at that radius has no hole left in it and renders as three smudges. The dot is drawn solid so that it reads as a dot, and that is the whole exception.

Exception 2 — the cap is the dot

ic-alert, ic-info and ic-help each end in a zero-length path.

ic-alert
ic-info
ic-help
<path d="M12 16.4h.01"/>

A path that goes nowhere, drawn with a round cap, is a round dot the width of the stroke. In spirit this is the opposite of an exception: it keeps the one weight rather than adding a filled circle beside it. It is listed because the geometry looks like a mistake to anyone reading the sprite.

09.7

Where the vocabulary is under strain

four rulings, made here so they are not made at a call site

Four places where the law had to be applied rather than merely quoted. Each is settled below. A ruling written on a sheet is a decision; the same ruling improvised in a component is a defect waiting for its second instance.

1 — The chevron family is not four meanings

One drawing, four rotations, one meaning: disclosure.

closed
open
collapse
back

Direction is a parameter of one meaning, not a second meaning, which is why four names do not break the corollary. The rule that follows: a disclosure caret rotates, it never swaps drawing — and it is never ic-more.

2 — Plus and minus name an operation, not an object

The glyph says what happens; the control says what it happens to.

‹zoom›
one less · one more
one more attachment

This is the same rule that governs size — set by the row, not by the glyph. The pair is never adjacent to a third meaning, and nothing else in the seventy means increase or decrease, so the corollary holds.

3 — Fit is a camera move, not a frame

ic-frame means the boundary drawn around a selection. Fitting the board to the viewport creates no boundary.

‹zoom›
the ruling

Fit ships as a word. The vocabulary has no drawing for it and does not borrow one, exactly as Save did not in §09.1. The worded control is a plain .btn.ghost.sm — 24px, --r-10. This sheet owns the vocabulary; sheet 10 owns the ladder, and a control's height and radius are ladder business. A 24px word standing beside 26px icon buttons is not a defect to be sized away: it is what the ladder looks like when a word and a glyph sit in one cluster.

4 — Two hexagons, and only one of them may be on the board

A silhouette collision, resolved by scope rather than by redrawing.

ic-agent · i-12
ic-skill · i-12
ic-agent · i-20
ic-skill · i-20

At .i-20 the interior separates them — a core dot against three seams. At .i-12, the size the kind glyph actually ships at, it very nearly does not. ic-agent is a node kind and load-bearing under Decision 2, so ic-skill yields: it is allowed in the programs list and never on the board or in the kind legend.

Handback. Three items for the substrate, two still open. (1) Closed. The reference sheet's zoom cluster used to render ic-frame under aria-label="Fit". Ruling 3 replaced it with the worded control everywhere it was drawn — _core.html, sheet 01 and sheet 08 — and _core.html now carries a real .btn.ghost.sm rule so no sheet needs an inline size. The alternative — drawing an ic-fit — still needs a justification written before the drawing. (2) ic-skill and ic-agent share a hexagonal silhouette at .i-12. Ruling 4 confines the collision rather than resolving it; if programs and agents ever appear in one list, one of the two must be redrawn, and it cannot be the node kind. (3) The board toolbar ships five tools and the sprite now draws four of them — Decision 8 closed Erase; Decision 9 closed Pan. Arrow remains worded in §09.5; an arrow that is not ic-arrowright would close it, and it still owes the five conditions of §09.8 before it is drawn.

09.8

Drawing a seventy-seventh

the bar, stated so it can be met

A new symbol is justified by a named surface that has nothing to draw — the same standard the twelve Sequence additions met, and the same one the fifteen before them met. Before you draw: the set already covers the six node kinds, the canvas, files, functions, folders, branches, commits, shells, threads, models, programs, runs, claims, chrome, settings and every chevron. Decision 8 closed four whiteboard gaps; Decision 9 closed Pan and Box (ic-rect) against the table below — but the bar itself does not move: Arrow is still worded in §09.5, and it remains the standing worked example rather than a gap to be filled at a call site. Ten more are held in §09.5 with meanings already written. If the thing you need a mark for is a state, it is a verdict badge or a provenance tag, and those carry a word.
