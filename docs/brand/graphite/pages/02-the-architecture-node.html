Sheet 02 · The board

The architecture node card

One chassis, six kinds, and a single sentence that governs the whole of it: tone and elevation buy the separation, so the border never has to. A card that is the same colour as the ground it lies on has only its outline left to argue with, and an outline loud enough to win that argument has stopped being chrome and become a claim.

Everything below is the body. The icon, the silhouette, the border treatment and the tone step that say which kind of thing this is are Decision 2 and are specified on the kinds sheet. This sheet specifies what is identical for all six: the surface, the elevation, the resting border, the radius and padding, the type ramp, where evidence and counts sit, and how tall the card is allowed to be.

02.1

The chassis

--surface-2 · --e1 · --edge · --sp-10

A node card is a filled object lying on a plane. It carries a surface (--surface-2, reached through --arch-tone), an elevation (--e1, the card rung), and a border that is --edge in every resting state. Its width is fixed at --arch-card-w because a board reads as a board when its columns line up; its height is not fixed, and 02.3 is about why.

02.2

Tone and elevation buy the separation

the border is never asked to carry it

The card sits three rungs of the surface ramp above the field — --bg-ground is the board, --bg-base, --surface-1 and --surface-2 is the card — and it carries --e1 on top of that. Three signals agree: a lighter plane, a shadow under it, a hairline around it. Three weak signals in agreement read as one solid object. One strong coloured line reads as an alarm.

One kind reaches that height by a different route, and it is worth knowing why. A box-shadow is painted on the border box, so on the agent — whose chamfered top-right corner is a clip-path, not a radius — it would lay a square shadow under a cut corner and undo the silhouette. .node.n-agent therefore computes box-shadow: none and its outer plate carries --e1-drop instead, a drop-shadow() of the same offset and blur, which follows what is actually painted. The height is identical; only the mechanism differs. The agent may not sit flat while the other five are lifted, because that would make elevation a sixth carrier of kind and Decision 2 spends exactly four.

This is the whole argument for Decision 2. A card with no tone step and no elevation has only its outline left, the outline has to be found against a near-black field, and the only way to make an outline that findable is to give it a hue — at which point the board is spending the verdict budget on categories, and a datastore is drawn in the same green that means passed.

Do
Datastore

Tone plus --e1. The border can stay at --edge and the hue budget is untouched.

Don't
Datastore

No tone, no elevation, so the border had to become a hue — and the hue it became is the one that means passed. This datastore has not passed anything.

What the card must hold, stated as requirements rather than as taste. Text is read, so text takes the text floor. A ring the reader is asked to find takes the non-text floor. The resting border is asked only to be seen, against a plane that is already three rungs lighter than the ground, and it is deliberately not held to a boundary threshold: a hairline strong enough to clear one on its own has become a claim.

Selection is the product's own voice and takes --accent at --w-rule plus a fill — selection outranks every other carrier and must be louder than the state it replaces, never quieter.

02.3

Height is content; the floor is only a floor

width is fixed · height is not

A board needs uniform width, and --arch-card-w already fixes it. It does not need uniform height, and it must not have one: a card with a summary is taller than a card without, a card with evidence is taller again, and that difference is a reading of the graph rather than noise in it. A one-line card must be a one-line card.

The three heights below are derivable from the specs in 02.1, so they are literals rather than placeholders. Minimum card: --sp-10 + a 14px header row + --sp-4 + --lh-12 + --sp-10, plus two hairline borders = 56px. Add one line of subtitle and it is 56 + --sp-4 + --lh-11 = 75px. Add the footer and it is 75 + --sp-4 + --sp-4 + a 16px .prov = 99px.

Handback — the floor's value. The rule this sheet specifies is that --arch-card-min-h may never exceed the natural height of the smallest legitimate card, or it is a fixed height wearing a floor's name. That smallest card measures 56px by the arithmetic above; the substrate declares the floor at 96px, which is within a hair of the fullest card and therefore flattens every shorter one. Every specimen on this sheet is drawn at its natural height so the specification can be seen. The token wants its value corrected to the minimum card, not the maximum one.
02.4

The four slots and the type ramp

Four slots, in one order, on every kind. .nd-hd is the icon and the kind tag. .nd-t is the node's own name and is the only thing on the card set in --ink-1. .nd-s is one line of summary and is optional. .nd-ft is evidence and counts, and is pushed to the bottom of the card by margin-top:auto so it lands on the card's floor however tall the card grew.

02.5

Where evidence and counts sit

evidence left · counts right · both in the footer

Evidence is how the analyzer knows this node exists, and it is a .prov tag on the left of the footer. Traced means the analyzer followed it in the source. Declared means a manifest or a config said so and the source could not confirm it — that is --unknown, never --wont, because a claim nobody could check has not failed at anything. The two words match the edge proof vocabulary exactly, so a node and the connectors leaving it never disagree about what proof means.

A count goes on the right of the same footer, through .right, in mono at --t-10 and --ink-3. Every count on a node card is a placeholder. Members, callers, callees, functions, lines — the engine computes all of them, and a plausible number in a specimen is the exact failure Law 4 exists to prevent.

The footer is optional. A node the analyzer has nothing further to say about gets no footer, and is shorter for it.

Evidence is not a verdict. .p-measured and .p-declared carry --fits and --unknown, which are hues — and they are permitted here for the same reason the edge states are: proof is a claim about the world, which is precisely what Law 1 reserves hue for. At most one provenance tag per card. Two of them and the card has become a legend.
02.6

Connection handles are not painted

not connectable · not drawn

When the graph is not connectable, the card renders zero connection handles — not transparent ones, not zero-opacity ones, not ones behind a hover rule. None in the markup at all. A nub is an affordance, and an affordance for something the reader cannot do is a lie told eight times per node.

Where the card is connectable — draw mode, and only draw mode — the handles appear on entering that mode and leave with it. They are painted at --accent, because offering to make a connection is the product's own voice, and they are the only thing on the card that changes when the mode does.
