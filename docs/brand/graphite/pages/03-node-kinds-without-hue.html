Sheet 03 · Sequence extensions

Node kinds, and how they differ without colour

A node's kind is a category — what sort of thing the analyzer found. A node's state is a claim about the world — it passed, it is at risk, it is selected, it failed. Law 1 spends hue on claims, and there are never more hues than there are claims. A category is not a claim, so a category does not get a hue. Kind is carried instead by four non-chromatic channels: an icon, a silhouette, a border treatment and a step in the neutral tone ramp.

This is not a compromise made to accommodate colour vision. It is the stronger design on every axis that matters for a canvas — it survives being zoomed out past the labels, it scales past six categories where a hue budget cannot, it is legible to every reader, and it gives the accent back to selection outright. The retired scheme did none of those things, and it also, byte for byte, told the reader that every datastore had passed.

03.1

The kind hues are retired, and here is what they were saying

four of five were the status vocabulary

The board previously assigned one hue per kind. Four of those five hues were not merely similar to Sequence's status colours — they were the same values. A datastore was painted in the token that means passed. A topic was painted in the token that means warning; the warning token was in fact declared as an alias of the topic token, so the two could not have drifted apart even in principle. A service was painted in the accent, which Law 2 reserves for the product's own voice. An entry was painted in the muted token that means not known.

Below, each retired kind is drawn beside the status badge it collided with. Nothing has been approximated: the card and the badge beside it resolve to the same custom property.

The default layered arch layout flows the graph rightward — archLayout.ts:105 sets 'elk.direction': 'RIGHT' on the layered case, which is also the default — so datastores and topics collect downstream. The downstream half of every board therefore resolved to a field of passed and warning about nothing at all, and the one card that was genuinely selected competed with every service on screen for the same gold. The qualifier is load-bearing: the plain-English board is not laid out this way — graph/layout.ts:114 sets BOARD_ELK_OPTIONS['elk.direction'] to 'DOWN' — and there the same pile-up runs down the page instead of across it. The direction changes; the defect does not.

A reader cannot unlearn a verdict colour. Once green means --fits anywhere in the product, a green rectangle means it passed everywhere in the product, including on a rectangle that was only ever trying to say this is a table.

Why the hue was load-bearing, and why it no longer has to be. The retired card had --e0 in every state and sat almost exactly on the ground it was drawn on — no tone step, no elevation, no shadow. With separation unavailable from surface and light, hue was the only channel left, so the design had to spend it. The card now takes a real tone step (--arch-tone-*, off the graphite surface ramp) and --e1. Once a card is a raised object, its border can go quiet at --edge — and quiet borders are what freed the hue.
03.2

The four channels that carry kind

no colour token in the --arch-* namespace

Each channel answers at a different distance. The icon is read when the card is legible; the silhouette is read when it is not; the border treatment says what sort of evidence the boundary rests on; the tone carries containment depth first and separation second. They are declared once per kind and the card chassis is otherwise identical — a kind sets four custom properties and nothing else.

Ordered strongest to weakest. No pair of kinds may rest on the weakest alone.

03.3

Five kinds and one position, drawn

--arch-card-w · --arch-card-min-h is a floor

On the board field, at rest, with nothing selected and no verdict attached, each at the full --arch-card-w × --arch-card-min-h. There is not one hue between them. Every difference a reader can see here is a difference of shape, glyph, boundary or grey.

Corner radius alone is too weak a channel to carry six kinds, because the renderer is free to discard the number — see 03.4. So four of the six are separated by an outline a radius can express, and two by an outline it cannot: the module's folder tab and the agent's chamfer are drawn, and the datastore's cylinder keeps a real border while its top and bottom go elliptical. Nothing on this row is faked with a radius that will not render.

Each is a sentence about the thing — a datastore sits, a topic is a lane, a module is a container, an agent is not a deployed object.

A capsule end costs horizontal padding, and the two capsule kinds pay it. --r-full never paints 999px; it paints half the shorter side. On a card at the --arch-card-min-h floor that is half the card's height, and an arc that deep cuts inward far enough that a header glyph set at the card's own --sp-10 padding lands outside the painted shape. Entry and Topic are therefore drawn here at --sp-20 horizontal padding — exactly the trade Graphite already makes for .btn.pill, where the pill radius buys itself a wider horizontal pad than the square button. Entry pays it on the left end only, because its right corners are 0 and paint square; Topic pays it at both ends. The other four kinds do not pay it: the agent's chamfer is a fixed --sp-16 bite that clears the header line, and the datastore's top ellipse has already risen above the padding by the time the header starts. This now lives in the substrate, on .node.n-entry and .node.n-topic in _core.html, where it fixes every entry and topic specimen in the book at once. This page carries no inline padding override any more, and no sheet should write one.

Five kinds, then a hairline, then the one position — 03.7 states why entry may not stand as the sixth peer. The legend carries the silhouette and the glyph, and no colour chip, because there is no colour for a chip to show. Each silhouette is drawn at --arch-card-w ÷ 2 by --arch-card-min-h ÷ 2 — card aspect, half a card. The 26 × 18 chip this replaces was too short for its own radii to paint; see 03.4. Read the row as a vocabulary and not as a zoom level: the radii are scaled by the renderer, but the tab, the chamfer and the seam are token sizes and stay where they are, so a legend chip shows the shapes larger than a camera-scaled card of the same box would. What a camera actually does to them is 03.6's right-hand column. Module is in the legend. It is in the schema (parentId, memberIds) and it was being flattened onto Service before it ever reached the picture, which is how a board could contain modules and a legend could contain none.

03.4

The binding invariant

the test every board sheet is specified against

Every architecture node kind must remain distinguishable with all colour removed, at every size the silhouette is drawn, from the radii that actually paint. Render the board greyscale. If two kinds go ambiguous, the design is wrong — not the renderer, not the reader, the design.

The first clause is stronger than "do not rely on colour alone": it says a kind may not rely on colour at all. Anything a reader needs in order to tell a datastore from a topic must already be present in the achromatic picture, which means the greyscale render and the colour render carry exactly the same kind information — and the difference between them is precisely the set of claims the board is making.

The last two clauses are the ones that were missing, and their absence was this book's central defect. A declared radius is not a rendered radius. CSS computes one scale factor for the whole box — f = min(side length ÷ sum of the radii on that side), taken over all four sides — and multiplies every corner by it. Two consequences follow, and both bite on a board: any radius at or above half the box height paints as a stadium end, and on a short box two different tokens paint the same arc. Proving the invariant against the token list therefore proves nothing about the picture.

That is why every silhouette specimen on this page is at card aspect and at least half a card tall, and why the folder tab, the cylinder and the chamfer are drawn rather than declared — a radius that will not render is not a silhouette, it is a comment.

03.6

Every pair, and the channels that separate it

the invariant, specified rather than asserted

Every pair of kinds drawn in 03.3 is listed, and every row was re-derived against the outlines as they paint at the size drawn — not against the token list. The middle column names the channels that separate that pair on the full card. The right-hand column names what is left below half a card, which is the honest floor: at that scale the folder tab is 6px, the chamfer 8px and the cylinder's arc 7px, and below it those three features and the dash patterns stop reading. What survives further out is the coarse outline — round-ended, moderately rounded, or near-square — plus tone, plus the icon.

Two properties hold across the whole table and both are requirements, not observations: every pair differs in at least three of the four channels on the card, and every pair differs in both icon and silhouette. One property is reported rather than required, because it is what the geometry gives: one pair — service and datastore — falls to the icon alone below the floor. They share a tone, they share a border style, their weights snap together on a 1× display, and the only thing between their outlines is the rise of the cylinder's ellipse. That is a statement about level of detail, not a failure; it is the reason the icon is ranked the strongest channel in 03.2 and the reason the board must keep drawing it.

Seven of the fifteen pairs sit on the floor of three and eight clear it. Two rows are worth reading twice. Datastore / agent is the only pair the border separates on neither style nor weight — both are solid --w-struct — which is the price the substrate paid for drawing the agent as a painted plate, since a plate cannot carry a dash pattern; icon, silhouette and tone carry it instead. Service / datastore is the pair that used to be two rounded rectangles differing by a border weight, which is why the datastore now gets the most literal shape in the set: flat sides, an elliptical top and bottom, and the seam that is the lower arc of that top ellipse. Even so it is the pair that loses its outline first.

Weight is the channel that can fail, and the specification accounts for it. A --w-struct border is 1.5px, and 1.5px snaps to 1px on a 1× display — where it becomes indistinguishable from --w-hair. Read the middle column with the weight term struck out: every pair still differs in icon and silhouette, and no pair falls below two channels — service / datastore is the pair that lands on that floor. Weight is a reinforcement in this design and never a carrier. No new kind may be added whose only separation from an existing kind is weight.
The level-of-detail ladder, stated rather than implied. The ladder is about the camera, which scales every feature together, and not about the legend chip, which keeps the tab, the chamfer and the seam at token size while the renderer scales only the radii. At the full card every channel is readable. At half a card — a card the camera has taken to 80 × 48 — the outline still separates all six, and border weight is already gone. At half a card those three features are already at 6px, 8px and 7px respectively. Below half a card they fall under that, the dash and dot patterns break up, and the outline collapses to three coarse groups: round-ended (entry, topic), moderately rounded (service, module, datastore), near-square (agent). Tone and the icon are what remain, and where tone is equal the icon is alone. One caveat travels with that: in light, Graphite separates elevation by shadow rather than by tone and surfaces 2 through 4 resolve to one value, so the tone term in the right-hand column goes to zero there and more pairs land on the icon. Light is deferred under Decision 1, and this is recorded as a consequence to be checked when it is picked up, not tuned now. Below the icon's own floor nothing separates kinds at all — that is a zoom at which the board is no longer being asked what anything is.
03.7

Entry is a position, not a kind

--arch-sil-entry is a modifier on the base rectangle

Kind answers what a node is. Entry answers where the reader comes in — it is the node the board is entered through, and any kind can occupy it. A service that nothing upstream calls is an entry; make one call to it from inside the scope and it is a service again, without having changed what it is. That is why entry is not the sixth peer in the list, and why its silhouette is a modifier: the left end is opened into a capsule and the right end is squared off against it, so the shape says things arrive here from off-board and go on from that edge. Both ends are written as what paints — --r-full 0 0 --r-full. The token used to name --r-14 on the right, which on a 160 × 96 card is scaled by f to under a pixel and thrown away; it now says square, because square is what ships.

03.8

Why this is better than hue, not a compromise

four arguments, none of them accessibility alone

It holds when the labels are gone

A canvas spends most of its life zoomed out. Type has a floor; shape does not.

Six silhouettes at half a card — card aspect, 80 × 48 — with no glyph, no label and no colour, and they still sort into six groups. Half a card is the floor rather than an arbitrary legend size: it is the scale at which the folder tab, the chamfer and the cylinder's arc are still a visible step, and below it 03.6's right-hand column takes over. Hue would survive further out than this — but hue at any size is the one thing the design is not allowed to spend, because a green node and a passing verdict are the same object.

It is legible to every reader

The colour-blind case is a consequence, not the motivation.

Red–green colour vision deficiency is common enough that a scheme separating a datastore from a topic by green versus amber is a scheme some readers simply do not receive. Under the invariant this cannot happen by construction, and it cannot regress by accident either: the greyscale render is the same test the design already has to pass, so the accessibility property is not a second thing anyone has to remember to check.

It scales past six

The hue budget is closed. The shape space is not.

Law 1 fixes the hue budget at the number of claims, and Sequence's claims are already spent: --fits, --spills, --wont, --unknown, --info, plus the accent. A hue-coded kind scheme is therefore full at five and every kind after that has to borrow a meaning from a verdict. A silhouette-and-icon scheme costs one glyph and one shape per kind and takes them from a namespace nothing else is using.

The price of a seventh kind under the retired scheme was a colour the reader already knew the meaning of. The price under this one is a drawing.

It gives selection the accent outright

Selection outranks every other carrier and must be louder than what it replaces.

03.9

What a node is still allowed to be coloured for

state, and only state

Hue is not banned from the board. It is reserved — and the reservation is what makes it work, because a coloured node on a board of achromatic nodes is now unambiguous. Colour on a node means the node is doing or has been found to be something. It never means the node is a something.

Four coloured objects on a board of achromatic cards, and every one of them is a claim about the world that the reader can act on. Under the retired scheme these four would have been competing with every card behind them.

Do

Colour a node when it has a verdict, a risk, an error, a live run state, or the selection. Attach the word beside the colour, as sheet 05 requires — a verdict badge contains the verdict as text.

Don't

Colour a node because of what it is. Colour a node's border, its header rule or its kind label as chrome. Reassign a --viz-* series colour to a kind — the viz namespace is for series, and a kind is not a series.

what an implementation has to satisfy

Identical for every kind. Kind sets four custom properties and nothing else.

/* module tab, cylinder seam and agent

The outline ink is --arch-line, which rests at --edge for every kind in every theme. It exists so that hover and selection repaint one outline whether that outline is a CSS border or a painted plate. A kind that reaches for a border colour has left the design.
