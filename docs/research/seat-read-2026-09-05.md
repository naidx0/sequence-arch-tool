## The chart, in a person's words

A learner asks *"teach me how bigram_counts.py works, step by step"* and gets a picture of **three
boxes and two arrows**. `bigram_counts.py` sits in the middle and is the highlighted one. `nn_bigram.py`
and `sampling.py` sit either side, each with an arrow pointing **into** it. Under the picture: *"bigram_counts.py
and what it connects to, from the scanned graph."*

**Checked against the scan, edge by edge**, the repository has exactly two edges among these files:

```
file:nn_bigram.py  -> file:bigram_counts.py   (import)
file:sampling.py   -> file:bigram_counts.py   (import)
```

The chart draws those two, in that direction, and nothing else. Every one of its three items carries
a `nodeId` that exists. **Nothing is invented and nothing is missing.**

What a person actually learns from it: the file being taught is the one the other two *depend on* —
the counts are built here and read there. That is true, it is the right thing to notice first, and
the arrows point the way round that says it.

**The honest limit:** it is a small, plain picture, and for a three-file repository it is close to
the whole repository. It has not been seen rendered — the browser pane does not composite this app
(viewport 0×0 until forced, stale click rects after), so this is read from the chart body the bench
now records, not from pixels. **The pixel read is still outstanding.**

## And the finding under all of it: `buildQueue` misses natural phrasing

The first turn asked *"teach me how the bigram counts work"* and got **no concept, no chart, an empty
queue** — on a graph containing `file:bigram_counts.py`. Rewording to *"teach me how
bigram_counts.py works"* produced the whole lesson.

`buildQueue` seeds from nodes the ask NAMES, or from the whole repo if the ask says "this repo". *"the
bigram counts"* names the file in every way except punctuation and matches neither. A learner does not
type filenames with underscores and extensions; that is exactly the phrasing the mode exists to serve.

**This is the single highest-value fix left in teach mode.** Everything downstream — concept, derived
chart, advance — is already working and is gated behind an ask that names a file the way the scanner
spells it.

## Why `index.ts` is missing, answered three ways

**It is not a cap and not a chart miss. The scan does not have the edge.**

```
SCAN edges INTO brief.ts: 4
   cli.ts, repoServer.ts, brief.test.ts, scope-ddl.test.ts   (all kind: import)
```

The chart drew **all four the scanner has**. `index.ts` reaches `brief.ts` by
`export { renderBrief, … } from './brief.js'` — a **re-export**, and the scanner emits no edge for
one. Reproduced on a three-file fixture: `import` produces an edge, `export … from` produces none.

**That is a grounding gap in the graph itself, not in the drawing**, and it is worth more than this
chart: every claim the product makes about who depends on what is missing re-exports, and a barrel
`index.ts` is exactly where a codebase concentrates them. It needs its own change with its own
prediction — the blast radius is every edge count in the product — so it is recorded here and not
slipped into this work.

**The check-in naming three of four IS a cap**, mine: `deriveCheckIn` takes `dependents.slice(0, 3)`
so a question stays readable. Four names in one sentence reads as a list; three reads as a choice.

**And the cap bit somewhere I had not noticed.** `brief.ts` has one OUTBOUND edge, and the chart
shows none — `MAX_NEIGHBOURS` is 4 and the loop stops at four links, so the four inbound edges
filled the budget before the outbound one was reached. The picture therefore says what `brief.ts` is
used BY and never what it uses, purely because of graph edge order. That is a bias, not a bug — the
chart is still true — but a reader would reasonably assume the file depends on nothing, and it
depends on one thing. Worth fixing by reserving a slot for each direction rather than filling
first-come.
